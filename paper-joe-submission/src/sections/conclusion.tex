\section{Conclusion}
Under a fixed subject-separated MIT-BIH evaluation, the Morlet CNN--Conformer achieved the highest accuracy, macro-F1, and weighted-F1 among the four architectures evaluated in this study. The same analysis also identified the result that most constrains translation: performance remained concentrated in the Normal and VEB classes, while SVEB and Fusion F1-scores were near zero and could not be recovered by a simple post-hoc threshold shift. The study therefore supports the hybrid representation as the strongest configuration within this controlled benchmark, not as a clinically validated arrhythmia detector. Its broader value is the auditable evaluation framework---including fixed record separation, archived predictions, comparator runs, class-wise diagnostics, and explicit post-hoc calibration limitations---that can be reused to test imbalance-aware objectives, longer rhythm context, multi-lead inputs, and external cohorts. Progress toward clinician-in-the-loop use will require improved rare-class performance, independently selected operating points, external validation, and prospective assessment of how model outputs affect review decisions.
