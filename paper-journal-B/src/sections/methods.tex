\section{Methods}
\subsection{Data sources and cohort construction}
The analysis used the 48 half-hour, two-lead Holter recordings provided by the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database and harmonised every beat label under the Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) EC57 taxonomy to enable comparison with recent open benchmarks.\cite{Goldberger2000PhysioBank,Kim2024FortyFive} Each subject was assigned to a single split, producing non-overlapping training, validation, and test folds so that individuals never appeared in multiple cohorts. Corrupted segments and rhythm transitions lacking consensus annotations were removed, yielding 110,919 labeled beats. To mimic telemonitoring workloads, the native class imbalance was preserved, and 20\% of the examples from every arrhythmia subtype were reserved for the held-out evaluation cohort described in \autoref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Signal conditioning and representation learning}
Signals were left in the form distributed through PhysioNet to keep the benchmark comparable, so no additional band-pass or baseline wander filtering was applied beyond the acquisition settings. R-peak locations and beat annotations were parsed from the PhysioNet \texttt{.atr} files, after which 187-sample windows centered on each fiducial were extracted and concatenated into three-beat sequences, matching the public preprocessing script (\texttt{preprocess\_data.py}). Each window was transformed into a complex Morlet wavelet scalogram spanning 32 scales, and the magnitude component was retained to form time--frequency images that emphasize multiresolution morphology while preserving temporal alignment. The scalograms plus metadata were written to HDF5 containers (\texttt{preprocessed\_data\_h5\_raw/}). Amplitude normalization was deferred to the dataset-construction stage so that fold-specific statistics could be applied consistently by \texttt{DataLoader.py}. No augmentation such as jittering, polarity inversions, or synthetic resampling was introduced in the experiments reported here. All figures derived from these representations were exported at 300~dpi with 85~mm or 170~mm widths to satisfy BioMedical Engineering OnLine image specifications.

\subsection{Neural architecture and training procedure}
The classifier combines a shallow convolutional stem with Conformer blocks that interleave macaron feed-forward layers, multi-head self-attention with relative positional embeddings, and depthwise convolutions for local refinement. Hyperband explored embedding sizes (64 or 128), head counts (4 or 8), kernel widths (7 or 11), dropout rates between 0.1 and 0.3, and one or two Conformer blocks before selecting the 128-dimensional, 8-head, 11-sample configuration stored under \texttt{Research\_Runs/final\_run\_Main\_Model\_20250824\_154136}. Every model was trained with AdamW (learning rate $1\times10^{-5}$) for 20 epochs using class-weighted sparse categorical cross-entropy on NVIDIA-class GPUs controlled by \texttt{run\_final\_evaluation.py}. The emphasis on lightweight attention blocks mirrors BioMedical Engineering OnLine reports that pair residual attention with artifact-aware preprocessing in other sensing domains.\cite{Chen2024SuperResolution,Qiu2025Attention,Sugiarto2025GroundReaction}

\subsection{Baselines and ablation strategy}
To contextualise performance, the study reproduced two strong baselines: a convolutional recurrent network patterned after the CNN-LSTM specification reported in recent ambulatory arrhythmia studies and an attention-only Transformer tuned to match the Conformer parameter count.\cite{Fira2025SingleChannel,Kirkbas2025CommonMatrix} Both alternatives ingested identical scalograms, shared the optimizer schedule, and were trained through the same automation hooks. Additional ablations such as disabling depthwise convolutions or class weighting are scaffolded in the repository but are not reported here because they remain incomplete.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics and statistical analysis}
Model selection prioritised macro-averaged F1 to balance sensitivity across arrhythmia types, while accuracy, weighted F1, and one-vs-rest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) provided complementary summaries. \texttt{Evaluator.py} produced the held-out metrics, confusion matrix, and ROC as well as precision--recall plots directly from the stored predictions, ensuring that every number in the manuscript can be regenerated through a single command. Five-fold cross-validation on the 38 training records yielded mean and standard-deviation accuracy values via the automated \texttt{run\_kfold\_evaluation.py} routine, offering a coarse measure of subject-level variability. No additional bootstrapping, McNemar testing, or saliency aggregation was executed beyond these scripted evaluations, so the reported numbers represent point estimates grounded in the released artifacts.
