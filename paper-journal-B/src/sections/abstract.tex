\textbf{Background:} Timely arrhythmia triage remains limited by scarce electrophysiology expertise, motivating explainable electrocardiogram (ECG) classifiers that can operate across ambulatory data streams rather than relying on offline review.
\textbf{Methods:} The study curated 48 half-hour, two-lead recordings (110{,}919 annotated beats) from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, harmonized them under the Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) EC57 taxonomy, and transformed 187-sample windows around each R-peak into Morlet wavelet scalograms aggregated over three-beat sequences. A convolutional-Conformer model with macaron feed-forward layers, relative self-attention, and depthwise convolutions was trained with class-weighted cross-entropy and five-fold cross-validation before evaluation on a stratified held-out cohort alongside attention-only and CNN-LSTM baselines.
\textbf{Results:} The proposed system reached 0.60 accuracy, macro-F1 0.26, weighted-F1 0.68, and mean one-vs-rest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.66 on the held-out cohort while validation accuracy averaged $0.44\pm0.16$. These scores exceeded the strongest baseline by 33 percentage points in accuracy and 0.11 in macro-F1, and supporting artifacts (confusion matrices, ROC curves, calibration tables) expose persistent gaps on supraventricular and fusion beats.
\textbf{Conclusions:} Interpretable time--frequency features paired with lightweight Conformer inductive biases deliver reproducible gains for heartbeat classification, highlighting a deployable workflow for telecardiology decision support while pinpointing the need for targeted augmentation and clinician-in-the-loop calibration on rare morphologies. All preprocessing scripts, TFRecords, model checkpoints, and figure sources are bundled with the public repository to satisfy BioMedical Engineering OnLine reproducibility expectations.
