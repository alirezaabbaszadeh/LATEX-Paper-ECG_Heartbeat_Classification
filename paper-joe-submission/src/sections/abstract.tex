\textbf{Background:} Specialist shortages slow timely electrocardiogram interpretation, creating demand for reproducible arrhythmia classifiers that integrate with telemonitoring workflows. \textbf{Methods:} We processed the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database (48 half-hour records, 110{,}919 beats) into three-beat Morlet scalogram sequences and trained a lightweight Conformer--CNN with class-weighted cross-entropy on 38 development records drawn from the 45 non-paced subjects. Hyperband tuning, five-fold cross-validation, and evaluation on a held-out MIT-BIH test cohort comprising 7 subjects (15{,}573 beats) were orchestrated by the shared preprocessing, training, and evaluation scripts included in this repository. \textbf{Results:} The champion model reached 0.60 accuracy (95\% confidence interval [CI] 0.59--0.60), macro-F1 0.26 (95\% CI 0.26--0.27), and weighted-F1 0.68 (95\% CI 0.67--0.68) on the test split. One-vs-rest AUCs measured 0.90 (95\% CI 0.89--0.91) for normal beats, 0.84 (95\% CI 0.84--0.85) for ventricular ectopy, and 0.31 (95\% CI 0.29--0.34) for fusion beats, while cross-validation accuracy averaged $0.44\pm0.16$. Diagnostic ROC, precision--recall, and confusion-matrix plots highlighted persistent gaps on supraventricular and fusion beats that inform threshold selection for clinician oversight. \textbf{Conclusions:} Conformer encoders paired with Morlet representations deliver measurable gains over attention-only, CNN-LSTM, and feature-engineered baselines on MIT-BIH, yet rare-class sensitivity remains a bottleneck. The open-source pipeline and archived \texttt{Research\_Runs} artifacts provide a transparent starting point for augmentation, calibration, and prospective validation studies.
