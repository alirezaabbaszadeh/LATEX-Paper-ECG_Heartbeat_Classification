\section{Results}

\subsection{Held-out cohort and class imbalance}
The final evaluation was performed once on a subject-separated hold-out comprising 15{,}573 beats from seven MIT-BIH records that were not used for hyperparameter selection or model fitting. The test distribution was strongly imbalanced: 12{,}061 beats (77.5\%) were normal (N), 2{,}913 (18.7\%) were ventricular ectopic beats (VEB; V), 383 (2.5\%) were fusion beats (F), 214 (1.4\%) were supraventricular ectopic beats (SVEB; S), and only two belonged to the unknown/unclassifiable category (Q). This distribution is important for interpreting the aggregate scores: a model can achieve a relatively high weighted metric while still failing on clinically relevant minority rhythms. Five-fold evaluation across the 38 development records yielded an accuracy of $0.44\pm0.16$, indicating substantial between-split variability and motivating the class-specific analyses below.

\subsection{Performance relative to evaluated baselines}
On the held-out cohort, the proposed CNN--Conformer achieved an accuracy of 0.60 (95\% confidence interval [CI] 0.59--0.60), macro-F1 of 0.26 (95\% CI 0.26--0.27), and weighted-F1 of 0.68 (95\% CI 0.67--0.68). These were the highest values for all three model-level metrics among the four architectures evaluated under the same test split (Table~\ref{tab:test-metrics}). The attention-only network---the strongest competing neural model by held-out accuracy---reached 0.27 accuracy, 0.15 macro-F1, and 0.38 weighted-F1. The corresponding absolute differences were therefore 0.33, 0.11, and 0.30, respectively. CNN--LSTM and feature-engineered comparators reached accuracies of 0.18 and 0.14. The model-level advantage was not uniform across every class-specific discrimination metric; for example, CNN--LSTM produced a slightly higher VEB AUC than the proposed model. We therefore interpret the comparison as evidence of better overall balance across the evaluated metrics rather than across-the-board superiority for every rhythm class.

\subsection{Class-specific discrimination and error structure}
Performance separated sharply by rhythm class. Normal beats had precision 0.95, recall 0.60, and F1 0.74, while VEB achieved precision 0.48, recall 0.70, and F1 0.57. Their one-vs-rest AUCs were 0.90 (95\% CI 0.89--0.91) and 0.84 (95\% CI 0.84--0.85), respectively. By contrast, SVEB had F1 0.00 and fusion beats F1 0.01; the fusion AUC was 0.31 (95\% CI 0.29--0.34). The Q category contained only two test beats and is therefore reported for completeness but not used for substantive class-level interpretation.

The confusion structure makes the minority-class limitation more concrete. Of 214 SVEB beats, 151 (71\%) were assigned to the normal class and 51 (24\%) to VEB. Of 383 fusion beats, 343 (90\%) were assigned to VEB. These errors show that the principal limitation is not simply a small reduction in overall accuracy: under this split, rare rhythms are systematically absorbed into the much more prevalent normal or ventricular categories. Figure~\ref{fig:confusion} presents both absolute counts and row-normalised error proportions, while Figures~\ref{fig:roc} and~\ref{fig:pr} show the corresponding one-vs-rest discrimination curves.

\subsection{Post-hoc threshold and calibration analyses}
Threshold sweeps on the stored held-out probabilities did not convert the weak minority-class scores into balanced detectors. For SVEB, the maximum observed one-vs-rest F1 was 0.06 at a threshold of 0.09, with precision 0.03 and recall 0.90. For fusion beats, the maximum F1 was 0.05 at a threshold of 0.00, with precision 0.02 and recall 1.00. Thus, the apparent recall gains were obtained at the cost of very high false-positive burden. These sweeps are descriptive post-hoc analyses of the held-out predictions rather than independently validated operating-point selection.

An internal calibration analysis of the same stored probabilities further showed heterogeneous confidence behaviour. Before temperature scaling, the 15-bin expected calibration error (ECE) was 0.417 for normal beats and 0.089 for VEB, with Brier scores of 0.289 and 0.121, respectively. The fitted global temperature ($T=1.050$) changed mean negative log-likelihood only marginally, from 1.047989 to 1.047731. Because the temperature parameter was fitted on the held-out test cohort itself, these values are presented only as an internal diagnostic and not as independent evidence of calibration generalisability.
