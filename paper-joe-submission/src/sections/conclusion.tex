\section{Conclusion}
On unseen MIT-BIH records, the Morlet CNN--Conformer produced the strongest aggregate results among the four evaluated architectures. That lead came from Normal and VEB; SVEB and Fusion remained effectively unresolved, and post-hoc threshold changes exchanged recall for very low precision. Taken together, the results support the model as a reproducible study comparator while leaving rare-rhythm classification unsolved. The fixed patient separation, common comparators, archived predictions, and class-wise diagnostics make the next experiments auditable. Clinical utility will require better minority-class learning, thresholds and confidence estimates selected independently of the test cohort, external validation, and prospective assessment with clinicians.
