\section{Conclusion}
On unseen MIT-BIH records, the Morlet CNN--Conformer ranked first among the four evaluated models. It had the highest accuracy, macro-F1, and weighted-F1. That advantage was concentrated in Normal and VEB; SVEB and Fusion were rarely recognised, and changing their thresholds produced high recall only at very low precision. The model is therefore a useful comparator for this fixed protocol, not a solution to five-class beat recognition. The immediate test is whether additional rhythm context or lead information changes the two dominant rare-class error paths. Minority-class objectives chosen on development data and evaluation in an external cohort should follow before any prospective clinical assessment.
