Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our original research manuscript, \textbf{``Record-Level Evaluation of a Morlet CNN--Conformer for Five-Class ECG Beat Classification,''} for publication in \textit{Revista Portuguesa de Cardiologia}.

Automatic electrocardiogram (ECG) beat classification and arrhythmia screening are cornerstone capabilities for modern cardiovascular telemonitoring, wearable cardiac devices, and hospital telemetry. However, common methodological pitfalls---notably random beat-level data splitting and opaque summary metrics---often mask severe model failures on critical minority arrhythmias (such as supraventricular ectopic beats and fusion beats).

In this work, we present a patient-independent, record-level evaluation of a 32-scale Morlet continuous wavelet transform coupled with a Conformer sequence encoder on 45 MIT--BIH records. While the Conformer architecture achieved superior aggregate performance (accuracy 0.60, macro-F1 0.26, weighted-F1 0.68) compared to CNN-attention, CNN-LSTM, and baseline CNN models, our failure-aware analysis shows that performance gains were concentrated almost exclusively in dominant classes (Normal and ventricular ectopic beats), with near-zero recognition of rare arrhythmias. We provide a thorough error matrix, threshold sensitivity, and calibration diagnostic to establish realistic boundaries for deep learning translation in clinical cardiology.
