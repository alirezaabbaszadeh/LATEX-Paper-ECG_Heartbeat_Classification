\textbf{Background:} Patient-specific morphology and severe class imbalance can mask poor minority-rhythm recognition in aggregate ECG classification scores. We tested a Morlet CNN--Conformer under subject-separated evaluation and examined class-specific failure modes. \textbf{Methods:} Forty-five non-paced MIT-BIH records were assigned to a fixed record-level split: 38 for development and seven held-out records containing 15{,}573 beats for final evaluation. Three adjacent beats were represented as 32-scale Morlet scalograms. The CNN--Conformer was compared with attention-only, CNN--LSTM, and feature-engineered baselines on the same split. \textbf{Results:} The CNN--Conformer achieved 0.60 accuracy (95\% CI 0.59--0.60), 0.26 macro-F1 (95\% CI 0.26--0.27), and 0.68 weighted-F1 (95\% CI 0.67--0.68), the highest values for these metrics among the evaluated architectures. The attention-only comparator reached 0.27, 0.15, and 0.38, respectively. Performance remained class dependent: Normal and ventricular ectopic beats achieved F1-scores of 0.74 and 0.57, whereas SVEB and Fusion achieved 0.00 and 0.01. Post-hoc threshold sweeps increased minority-class recall only at very low precision; five-fold development accuracy was $0.44\pm0.16$. \textbf{Conclusions:} The hybrid model improved aggregate performance relative to the controlled baselines under subject-separated testing, but rare-rhythm recognition remained the limiting result. The archived pipeline, predictions, and class-wise diagnostics provide a reproducible benchmark for imbalance-aware modelling and external validation, not evidence of autonomous clinical readiness.
