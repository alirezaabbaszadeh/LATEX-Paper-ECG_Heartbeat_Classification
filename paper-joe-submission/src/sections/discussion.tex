\section{Discussion}
Pairing convolutional feature extractors with Conformer blocks yielded the strongest performance across all tested architectures. On the held-out MIT-BIH cohort, the proposed model achieved accuracy of 0.60 (95\% confidence interval [CI] 0.59--0.60), macro-F1 of 0.26 (95\% CI 0.26--0.27), and weighted-F1 of 0.68 (95\% CI 0.67--0.68), clearly outperforming the attention-only, CNN-LSTM, and feature-engineered baselines while remaining below thresholds typically targeted for autonomous diagnostic use. F1-scores of 0.74 for normal beats and 0.57 for ventricular ectopic beats (VEB), with corresponding recalls of 0.60 and 0.70, indicate that the network captures dominant rhythms and VEB reasonably well, whereas supraventricular ectopic beats (SVEB) and fusion beats remain poorly detected despite class weighting. Together with the confusion matrix and ROC/precision--recall curves, these findings support positioning the system as a triage aide that prioritises candidate arrhythmic segments in high-volume telemetry streams, complementing remote monitoring initiatives that pair wearable sensing with clinician oversight\cite{tison2018passive,lu2024early}. Cross-validation accuracy of $0.44\pm0.16$ across 38 development records further highlights subject-level variability and motivates conservative deployment thresholds.

Calibration analyses performed on the archived test-set probabilities, using a 15-bin expected calibration error (ECE) and Brier scores, show that the dominant normal class is substantially overconfident (ECE 0.42, Brier 0.29) and VEB moderately better calibrated (ECE 0.09, Brier 0.12). Minority rhythms such as SVEB and fusion exhibit lower ECE values (0.10 and 0.10) but similar Brier scores (0.04--0.05), reflecting cautious yet weakly informative probabilities. These calibration trends reinforce that raw model scores should not directly drive therapeutic decisions; instead, they should be combined with post-hoc calibration and used as inputs to decision-support tools that keep clinicians in the loop.

Comparison with prior deep-learning studies reinforces this interpretation. While cardiologist-level accuracy has been reported for single-lead wearable signals using large residual networks and millions of labelled beats\cite{hannun2019cardiologist}, those systems often rely on simplified label groupings or record-wise splits that permit patient overlap, making their headline metrics not directly comparable to the present five-class, inter-patient evaluation on MIT-BIH. Within this stricter setting, our Conformer--CNN achieves moderate discrimination with a comparatively lightweight architecture while delivering clear gains over attention-only and CNN-LSTM baselines. These improvements align with earlier evidence that multi-scale feature fusion improves arrhythmia recognition in imbalanced datasets\cite{acharya2017deep,luz2016ecg,jenifer2024yolo,scarpiniti2024arrhythmia}. Our open-source pipeline therefore extends existing literature by packaging Conformer-based modelling into reproducible scripts suited for translational investigations and complements concurrent efforts on feature-efficient wearable deployments\cite{rahman2025resource} as well as Journal of Electrocardiology advances on residual-attention classification that motivate our inductive biases\cite{rahman2024residual}.

Targeted post-hoc analyses further examined whether decision-threshold tuning could recover minority-class performance. Treating SVEB and fusion beats as one-vs-rest detection problems and sweeping per-class thresholds on the stored test probabilities, the best attainable F1-scores reached only 0.06 for SVEB (threshold 0.09; precision 0.03, recall 0.90) and 0.05 for fusion beats (threshold 0.00; precision 0.02, recall 1.00). These trade-offs indicate that substantial gains in recall would be accompanied by clinically unacceptable false-positive rates and that rare rhythms should not be acted upon without human confirmation. In practice, this argues for restricted use of the model as a prioritisation tool---for example, to highlight episodes with high VEB burden or frequent abnormal predictions for review---rather than as a stand-alone arrhythmia detector.

\subsection*{Limitations}
Nonetheless, several limitations remain. Performance on fusion beats is weak across all evaluated architectures, mirroring the scarcity and annotation noise of this rhythm class. The present study also evaluates a single, legacy public dataset; prospective validation on contemporary multi-lead and wearable cohorts will be necessary to confirm generalisability\cite{attia2019artificial,ribeiro2020automatic,thapa2025generalizable}. We focused on single-lead representations derived from the first channel of each record and short three-beat sequences, which simplifies deployment but may under-use information present in additional leads and longer rhythm context. Moreover, class-weighted cross-entropy mitigated but did not eliminate the influence of label imbalance, and we did not retrain models with alternative objectives such as focal loss or explicitly cost-sensitive learning\cite{chawla2002smote,he2009learning}. Finally, all bootstrap confidence intervals for test-set metrics were obtained from a single held-out cohort of 7 subjects and should be interpreted as internal rather than population-level uncertainty estimates.

\subsection*{Future work}
Future work should pursue targeted data augmentation (e.g., physiologically plausible beat morphing and rhythm-level perturbations), semi-supervised or self-supervised pre-training on unlabelled Holter streams, and integration of rhythm context beyond three-beat windows. Incorporating alternative loss functions and post-processing strategies---such as focal loss, calibrated decision thresholds, or cost-sensitive optimisation aligned with clinical priorities---may further improve minority-class sensitivity without degrading overall accuracy. Extending the architecture to exploit longer temporal context and multi-lead inputs, and evaluating performance on contemporary wearable datasets, would clarify generalisability beyond MIT-BIH\cite{attia2019artificial,ribeiro2020automatic}. Finally, expanding evaluation to include decision-curve analysis, prospective clinician-in-the-loop simulations, and prospective calibration under dataset shift will be essential steps toward safe integration into telehealth and hospital monitoring workflows.
