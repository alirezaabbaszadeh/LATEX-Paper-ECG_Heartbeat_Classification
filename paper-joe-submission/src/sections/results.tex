\section{Results}

\subsection{Held-out cohort and class imbalance}
The final test cohort comprised 15{,}573 beats from seven MIT-BIH records that had not been used for hyperparameter selection or model fitting. Normal beats accounted for 12{,}061 observations (77.5\%) and ventricular ectopic beats (VEB; V) for 2{,}913 (18.7\%). The remaining classes were much smaller: 383 fusion beats (F; 2.5\%), 214 supraventricular ectopic beats (SVEB; S; 1.4\%), and two unknown/unclassifiable beats (Q). Normal and VEB together therefore represented nearly all of the cohort. Across the 38 development records, five-fold accuracy was $0.44\pm0.16$.

\subsection{Performance relative to evaluated baselines}
The CNN--Conformer had the highest values for all three aggregate measures on the common test cohort: accuracy 0.60 (95\% confidence interval [CI] 0.59--0.60), macro-F1 0.26 (95\% CI 0.26--0.27), and weighted-F1 0.68 (95\% CI 0.67--0.68) (Table~\ref{tab:test-metrics}; Figure~\ref{fig:model-comparison}). The attention-only network, which had the next highest test accuracy, yielded 0.27, 0.15, and 0.38, respectively. The absolute differences were 0.33 for accuracy, 0.11 for macro-F1, and 0.30 for weighted-F1. Accuracy was 0.18 for CNN--LSTM and 0.14 for the feature-engineered model. The class-specific AUC results did not follow the same ordering; for example, CNN--LSTM had a slightly higher VEB AUC than the CNN--Conformer.

\subsection{Class-specific discrimination and error structure}
Meaningful F1-scores were confined to Normal and VEB. For Normal, precision was 0.95, recall 0.60, and F1 0.74; the corresponding VEB values were 0.48, 0.70, and 0.57. One-vs-rest AUC was 0.90 (95\% CI 0.89--0.91) for Normal and 0.84 (95\% CI 0.84--0.85) for VEB. SVEB had an AUC of 0.67 but an F1-score of 0.00. Fusion had an AUC of 0.31 (95\% CI 0.29--0.34) and an F1-score of 0.01. The Q results are shown for completeness; two test beats are insufficient for class-level interpretation (Table~\ref{tab:class-metrics}; Figure~\ref{fig:class-performance}).

Most minority-class errors followed a small number of paths. Of 214 SVEB beats, 151 (71\%) were assigned to Normal and 51 (24\%) to VEB. Among the 383 fusion beats, 343 (90\%) were assigned to VEB (Figure~\ref{fig:error-structure}). \subsection{Post-hoc threshold and calibration analyses}
In the post-hoc threshold sweep, the highest observed one-vs-rest SVEB F1 was 0.06 at a threshold of 0.09 (precision 0.03; recall 0.90). For fusion beats, the highest F1 was 0.05 at a threshold of 0.00 (precision 0.02; recall 1.00). The increase in recall thus carried a large false-positive burden. These values are descriptive because the sweep was performed on the stored test predictions.

Before temperature scaling, the 15-bin expected calibration error (ECE) was 0.417 for Normal and 0.089 for VEB; their Brier scores were 0.289 and 0.121, respectively. A global temperature of $T=1.050$ changed mean negative log-likelihood from 1.047989 to 1.047731. The temperature was fitted on the test cohort, so this is an internal description of confidence behaviour rather than independent calibration validation.
