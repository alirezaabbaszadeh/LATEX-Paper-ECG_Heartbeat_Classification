\section{Extended Methods}
\subsection{Data Acquisition and Partitioning}
We accessed the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database through the repository's \texttt{download\_data.py} utility. The script mirrors the PhysioNet distribution and writes the raw waveforms into a cached directory so that repeated experiments reuse identical sources. After excluding paced-beat recordings, 45 records were retained for the AAMI-oriented workflow. A fixed record-level split with random seed 42 assigned seven records (106, 207, 208, 220, 228, 231, and 233) to the held-out test cohort and 38 records to the development pool. The held-out records contained 15{,}573 beats. Hyperparameter tuning and five-fold cross-validation were restricted to the development pool; the final selected configuration was then fitted on development data and evaluated on the fixed held-out cohort.

\subsection{Signal Conditioning and Feature Engineering}
Signal preparation begins with R-peak detection and beat-centric window extraction implemented in \texttt{preprocess\_data.py}. For every annotated beat, 187-sample segments centred on the R peak are extracted from the first ECG channel and transformed into Morlet wavelet scalograms using the PyWavelets \texttt{morl} kernel at 32 integer scales (1--32). Normalisation statistics are applied during dataset construction rather than permanently embedded in the cached scalograms. Subsequent batching via \texttt{create\_batched\_tfrecords.py} assembles triples of adjacent beats, encodes tensor metadata, and writes TFRecord shards for streaming input.

\subsection{Model Configuration and Optimisation}
The main architecture follows \texttt{ModelBuilder.py}. Two convolutional stem blocks (kernel sizes $[3,3]$ with 32 and 64 filters) precede one or two Conformer encoder blocks explored by Hyperband (embedding dimension 64 or 128, 4 or 8 attention heads, feed-forward expansion factor 2 or 4, kernel size 7 or 11, and dropout 0.1--0.3). Dropout is applied within the feed-forward, attention, and convolutional components. Neural models are compiled in TensorFlow~2 with AdamW (learning rate $1\times10^{-5}$), sparse categorical cross-entropy, and mini-batches of 128 sequences. Hyperband trials train for up to 20 epochs with early stopping and ReduceLROnPlateau callbacks. The selected configuration is subsequently trained for 20 epochs for the definitive held-out evaluation. No gradient clipping or exponential moving average is applied during the final training run. Mixed-float16 precision is enabled on compatible GPUs.

The repository archives configuration files, training histories, classification reports, and stored prediction probabilities under \texttt{Research\_Runs}, enabling the manuscript tables and diagnostic analyses to be traced back to run-level artifacts.

\subsection{Error Breakdown and Threshold Sweeps}
Class-specific trade-offs were examined after final prediction by sweeping decision thresholds on the stored held-out probabilities. Treating SVEB and Fusion as one-vs-rest detection problems, the maximum observed F1 was 0.06 for SVEB (threshold 0.09; precision 0.03, recall 0.90) and 0.05 for Fusion (threshold 0.00; precision 0.02, recall 1.00). These values show that increasing recall required a very large false-positive burden rather than recovering a balanced minority-class operating point. The confusion counts further show that 71\% (151/214) of SVEB beats were predicted as Normal and 24\% (51/214) as VEB, while 90\% (343/383) of Fusion beats were predicted as VEB. These analyses are post-hoc descriptions of the fixed held-out predictions and are not independently validated threshold-selection procedures.

\subsection{Internal Calibration Diagnostic}
Temperature scaling was also applied to the stored held-out probabilities. Before scaling, 15-bin ECE values were 0.417 for Normal, 0.098 for SVEB, 0.089 for VEB, 0.103 for Fusion, and 0.153 for Q. The fitted global temperature was 1.050, changing mean negative log-likelihood from 1.047989 to 1.047731. Because the temperature parameter was fitted on the held-out test cohort itself, this analysis characterises confidence behaviour in the current cohort only; it is not independent calibration validation and does not establish external generalisability.
