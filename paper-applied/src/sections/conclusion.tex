\section{Conclusion}
This work presented a two-stage waveform analysis pipeline that converts three-beat Morlet scalograms into arrhythmia predictions using a lightweight Conformer, yielding 0.60 accuracy and 0.26 macro-F1 on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database under the AAMI five-class protocol. Under this stringent inter-patient, record-level evaluation, performance remains modest in absolute terms but clearly surpasses the CNN-only, CNN-LSTM, and attention-only baselines packaged with the repository, highlighting the value of hybrid inductive biases even when minority classes remain difficult. Rather than claiming state-of-the-art performance, the study offers a transparent reference implementation that other groups can adopt or extend when benchmarking ECG classifiers on MIT-BIH.

The reproducibility-first design—shared preprocessing scripts, code, predictions, and released weights—aligns with open-access best practices. It mirrors recent efforts to couple interpretable modelling with resilient sensing pipelines across physiological domains.\cite{Jiang2025Hybrid,Stern2024InBed,Aslan2025Poincare} Future work will integrate uncertainty-aware calibration (e.g., temperature scaling), targeted augmentation and cost-sensitive loss formulations for supraventricular and fusion rhythms, and evaluation on additional ECG cohorts so that validations extend beyond a single public benchmark and closer to routine telemonitoring workflows.
