\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Database and record-level partition}
Experiments used the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database distributed through PhysioNet, which contains 48 half-hour, two-lead ambulatory ECG recordings sampled at 360~Hz\cite{goldberger2000physiobank,moody2001impact}. Following the AAMI-oriented preprocessing implemented in the repository, paced-beat recordings were excluded and 45 records were retained for model development and evaluation. A fixed record-level split (\texttt{test\_size}=0.15; random seed 42) assigned seven records (106, 207, 208, 220, 228, 231, and 233) to the held-out test cohort and the remaining 38 records to the development pool. The held-out records contained 15{,}573 beats and were excluded from hyperparameter selection and model fitting. Five-fold cross-validation was performed within the 38-record development pool, after which the selected configuration was fitted on the development records and evaluated on the held-out cohort. Table~\ref{tab:cohort-distribution} summarises the evaluation cohorts and the final test-set class support.

The split definition is reused consistently by the hyperparameter-tuning, cross-validation, and final-evaluation scripts so that the final test records do not migrate into the training pool between stages. Data acquisition is handled by \texttt{download\_data.py}, and the exact split identifiers and run artifacts are retained in the repository to make the evaluation path auditable.

\subsection{Beat representation and sequence construction}
The shared preprocessing pipeline (\texttt{preprocess\_data.py}) detects R peaks, extracts 187-sample windows centred on annotated beats from the first ECG channel (approximately 520~ms at 360~Hz), and computes Morlet continuous-wavelet scalograms with the PyWavelets \texttt{morl} kernel at 32 integer scales. Beat-centred localisation follows the general principles of classical QRS detection, while the wavelet representation provides a multiresolution description of transient morphology\cite{pan1985real,laguna1994automatic,addison2005wavelet,torrence1998practical,mallat1989wavelet}. Each beat is therefore represented as a $32\times187$ time--scale map.

Amplitude normalisation is applied during dataset construction using statistics derived from the relevant development data rather than being embedded in the cached raw scalograms. The TFRecord writer (\texttt{create\_batched\_tfrecords.py}) groups three temporally adjacent beats into each sequence and stores tensor shape and sequence metadata for streaming input. The three-beat context was chosen to expose the classifier to immediate rhythm context while retaining a short input sequence.

\subsection{CNN--Conformer and comparator models}
The primary classifier is implemented in \texttt{ModelBuilder.py}. Two-dimensional convolutional blocks first encode local patterns in the Morlet maps; the resulting sequence representation is processed by Conformer blocks that combine macaron-style feed-forward modules, multi-head self-attention, depthwise convolution, residual connections, layer normalisation, and dropout\cite{gulati2020conformer,vaswani2017attention,he2016deep,srivastava2014dropout,ba2016layer}. A final dense layer produces probabilities for the five AAMI categories N, S, V, F, and Q.

Three comparators were evaluated on the same held-out split: an attention-only neural baseline, a CNN--LSTM baseline in which recurrent modelling replaces the Conformer sequence encoder, and a feature-engineered baseline. These models are treated as controlled study comparators rather than a comprehensive survey of published architectures; the purpose is to isolate whether the selected hybrid representation improves the principal model-level metrics within the implemented pipeline.

\subsection{Optimisation and model selection}
Hyperparameter search is orchestrated by \texttt{run\_hyperparameter\_tuning.py} using Hyperband\cite{li2018hyperband}. The search covers embedding dimension, number of attention heads, convolution kernel width, dropout, and Conformer block count. \texttt{DataLoader.py} streams TFRecord batches and applies fold-specific normalisation. Neural models use AdamW with learning rate $1\times10^{-5}$, sparse categorical cross-entropy, and class weights computed from the development labels. Early stopping and learning-rate scheduling are used during Hyperband trials. The selected configuration is then trained for 20 epochs on the development data for the definitive held-out evaluation; the final run does not use early stopping or gradient clipping. Mixed-float16 execution is enabled on compatible GPUs, and the random seed, training history, model artifact, classification report, and raw test probabilities are archived under \texttt{Research\_Runs}.

\subsection{Evaluation and uncertainty}
The primary held-out metrics are accuracy, macro-F1, weighted-F1, class-wise precision, recall, F1, and one-vs-rest AUC. Macro-F1 is emphasised because it gives equal weight to each rhythm class and is therefore less dominated by the abundant normal beats than accuracy or weighted-F1. Five-fold development accuracy is summarised by its mean and standard deviation to expose variability across record partitions. For held-out point estimates, 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals are reported where archived in the study tables. ROC and precision--recall diagnostics are generated from the stored probabilities; precision--recall behaviour is considered particularly important for minority classes\cite{fawcett2006introduction,saito2015precision}.

Two additional analyses were performed after the definitive model predictions had been generated. First, one-vs-rest decision thresholds for SVEB and fusion beats were swept on the stored held-out probabilities to determine whether a simple operating-point change could recover minority-class F1. Second, per-class expected calibration error (ECE; 15 equally spaced confidence bins) and Brier scores were calculated, followed by single-parameter temperature scaling. Both analyses are explicitly post-hoc: the threshold sweep uses the held-out predictions, and the temperature parameter was fitted on the held-out cohort itself. They are therefore used to describe error and confidence structure, not to claim independently validated thresholds or calibration generalisability.

\subsection{Preparation of explanatory and data visualisations}
OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol) assisted with the LaTeX/TikZ layout of the workflow and summary figures. The tool did not generate or alter research data, predictions, or reported metrics. All displayed values are direct transcriptions of the archived study outputs documented in \texttt{qa/claim-ledger.md}, and the figures are deterministic from the included TikZ source. The original confusion-matrix, ROC, and precision--recall plots generated from the stored predictions remain unchanged as supplementary diagnostics. No general-purpose generative-AI tool was used to create a graphical abstract for submission.
